Train a character transformer on your GPU

A two-block, 113k-parameter decoder-only transformer (context 64, width 64, four heads) learning Alice's Adventures in Wonderland one character at a time, with Adam. Every step — embedding, causal attention, LayerNorm, GELU, loss, backward pass and update — is one graph in this project's IR, compiled to WGSL kernels by its own compiler and run in your browser, with no ML framework. The grey curve is PyTorch's run from the same initial weights on the same batches in float64, the dashed one PyTorch in float32; yours is float32 on your GPU. The report says what was checked and how.

Generated text, and the evidence that matters

After training, your GPU and this project's float64 interpreter each continue the prompt greedily (always the most likely next character), each from its own previous output, from the same float32 weights. The correctness evidence is how many of those characters agree. The text itself is illustrative: a model this small, trained for a minute, writes something only loosely like English, and no claim is made about its quality.
